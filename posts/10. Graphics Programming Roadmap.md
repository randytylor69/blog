# How to get into Graphics
[Computer Graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics) is a field in Computer Science that combines CS, math, and art - three things I have thoroughly enjoyed and am passionate about doing. This road map is by no means comprehensive and is open to contributions. 

&nbsp;

It's important to get a grasp of how much we've done to get to this point - I'd recommend spending a weekend shuffling through Reddit, Hacker News, and wikipedia rabbit holes to understand the end goal.

## 1. Work through a few years worth of [Advent of Code](https://adventofcode.com/) with C/C++
The *de facto* language of Computer Graphics is C/C++. The best way to learn them is through rigorous practice, and I picked Advent of Code (AOC 2025) as the "practice book" due to its leetcode-esque structure and un-leetcode-esque appearance. 

Instead of chipping through the verbose [Learn C++](https://www.learncpp.com/) or [The C Programming Language](https://colorcomputerarchive.com/repo/Documents/Books/The%20C%20Programming%20Language%20%28Kernighan%20Ritchie%29.pdf), one should instead consider performing a head-on collision with the first AOC puzzle they see, and immediately start utilizing the programming concepts, such as Arrays, Pointers, Data Types, Classes, File IO, etc. 

&nbsp;

## 2. Simple 2D Terminal Games
After knowing your way around C/C++, one could specialize their learning path into more diverse form of programming, such as building games that live in the terminal ([Snake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_(video_game_genre)), [Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris), or even [Flappy Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flappy_Bird)). Terminal is the birthplace of all modern entertainment, coding into the SSH has been such a joyful experience that it painlessly teaches the following essential concepts: rendering background, game loops, vector generation, collision detection, basic physics, etc.
