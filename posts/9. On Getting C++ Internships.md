## Work Experience

- Your work experience don't have to be in C++, but you need to understand working in larger codebases. You need to know how to organize things so the project can continue to grow. It's easy to scrape by with shitty organization on a 2k SLOC project but once you get over 5K the wheels have come off if you don't have things organized. 

&nbsp;

## Interview

- Talk to me about inheritance: virtual inheritance, diamond inheritance, default visibility. I especially like if you can give me examples of why i would want it and when I wouldn't.
  
- Tell me about your projects. Explain the scripts he'd written and the problems with scaling you had and had to solve. Problem solving experience and diligence in the face of adversity is way more valuable than knowing C++, I can teach you C++, I can't teach you to be a more dedicated problem solver.

- ⭐ I prefer to first ask some technicals (e.g. "Is vector faster than list?", "how does a hash map work?", etc); then ask around architectural things ("Talk me through a recent project" followed by discussing why you went with the design you did); and maybe sprinkle in a few "code review" samples where I bring out a code file with some class in it and see if you can identify the problems.
